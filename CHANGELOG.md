# Changelog

What changed in each release of WarEra Plus, newest first. Every number here was measured as it says; a synthetic page or market says so.

## 3.11.0

FLIP on a falling market. Since v0.27 went live, many cards read FLIP while the market listed the same stats for less: a value rested on up to a week of sales of these stats, and those sold dearer than the market sells now. The numbers below come from invented markets (`scripts/backtest-prices.mjs`), as it says; `scripts/backtest-flip.mjs` checks the same on the game's own sales with your key.

- **Sales brought down to today.** When an item's market fell, each sale a value rests on is brought down to today's level by the fall a day. The fall is read on prices already brought to the piece's stats, so a week that sold worse rolls later is no fall: over the item's newest 200 sales (a busier market's last 3 days), or over their last 3 days alone when those fell faster; from 8 sales on 3 days; 10% a day at most. A rising market lifts nothing: a false FLIP costs gold, a missed one only a chance. Every value of a piece is brought down: the market cards' FLIP and "sells", the picker's Flip, the Sell items hints and the Ledger's held pieces. The item's median over any stats and the game's average are not.
- **No FLIP above the offers it would sell against.** A piece bought to flip is listed again beside what the market lists now, and a buyer takes the cheaper of equal or better rolls. FLIP now also needs the second cheapest other offer on the page whose every stat is at least the piece's (the cheapest alone may be a slip) to sit your FLIP margin above the price, that offer taken before tax at the page's rate, as the sales are. The card's sell gain is against the lower of the two. Fewer than two such offers cap nothing.
- **Details say why.** One line under "Sells for", each part only when it applies: "trend −4%/day, sales brought to today · cap: 2nd offer 182 before tax". The card's own line is as it was.
- **On invented markets** (12 markets of each of four items, 20 offers each, 60 to 1,000 sales a week). An offer is a real flip when it is worth 10% more than its price after the item's median listed-to-sold wait, since a piece bought now sells only then. On the falling markets (3% or 6% a day; 15% in the last 24 or 48 hours; 15% four to seven days ago) FLIP was right 38.8% of the time before, 49.6% with sales brought to today and 58.3% with the cap too; it found 93.8%, 87.9% and 70.3% of the real flips. On flat markets it was right 70.6%, 71.0% and 79.9% of the time (found 84.1%, 81.6%, 64.2%), on rising ones 90.6%, 90.6% and 94.9% (found 51.3%, 50.8%, 38.0%). Judged by the worth at purchase instead of at resale, the falling markets' 58.3% would read 70.1%.
- **On the game's sales.** `WARERA_API_KEY=<key> node scripts/backtest-flip.mjs` replays the last 7 days of the items that fell most (or of the codes named), six hours at a time, with the sales and the offers the extension would have held, and counts how often each way's FLIP was right at resale, the tags that would have lost gold and the median gain. It only reads.

## 3.10.0

## 3.7.0

Ready for the game's v0.27, read on its dev server (dev.warera.io, client 0.27.0-beta) on 2026-10-17, and unchanged on today's v0.26. In v0.27 the equipment market becomes an auction house (`/market/equipments` leads to `/market/auctions`, the item offers and their API are gone), workers become NPC employees with tools, and a wooden case can drop cases. The auction code was checked against three auction cards and the Start an auction form read off the dev page (`fixtures/v027-auctions.json`), and its card readers read all 12 cards of the dev page's list (code, stats, start, max, countdown; the tax check fit the page's 10% on each). The built extension ran on the dev page too (the bar, the card lines, the Ledger, the picker's filter, the Start an auction hint); the case windows were read off the dev client's code, the dev account holding no case to open them with.

- **Auction cards.** Each card is read by its shape, not its words: the item's image and stats, the countdown, the start (or the current bid) and the max price, both before tax, and the two buttons, BUY NOW and BID, whose amounts include your country's market tax. SNIPE and FLIP judge buying now. A new tag, **BID**, marks a card whose next bid pays by its scraps or by what pieces of its stats sold for, and prints the most to bid for that margin before tax ("bid ≤ 28.59"), since bids are typed before tax and a bid in the last minute adds a minute, so the price is not final until the end. The game has no bid that rises by itself, so a bid placed earlier wins only if you are back at the end: BID shows in an auction's last 10 minutes, and before them the most to bid stays in Details (a fresh auction's next bid of 1.46 had read "+7,623%"). No tag and no gain rest on a card until its buy now and max price agree with the tax on the page (the card's own rate when the page prints none). A card is one piece with a priced BUY NOW and BID, its buy now its max price and a tax: a list, or a pager's numbers, is never taken for one. A card that fails the tax check is no peer of the others either, and its bid gets no before-tax figure. A card frame has no rarity border: a skinned piece's tier comes from its stat, as in the picker.
- **Start an auction.** With one piece picked, a line under the max price suggests a start, what its scraps fetch at the bids (so a lone bidder never takes it for less than dismantling gives), and a max, what pieces of its stats sold for, else the game's own estimate (the form's 100% while the average is not read), kept in the range the form prints. The game's estimate, read on 72 of 72 auctions: the item's average price times 1 + 5% of where its stats sit in their ranges (an epic sniper 123/16 at an 80.87 average: 82.404, the form's own number). **use** writes both prices; START AUCTION stays yours. With several pieces it says to pick one.
- **The bar on the auction page**, with Market, Craft and Ledger. When the page shows offer buttons and none can be read, the bar's head says "⚠ offers not read", with the game's build in its tooltip.
- **Tools, toolboxes, wooden cases in the Ledger.** A toolbox opens into a tool for an employee, not tradable and without a market price: tools stay out of every result and show apart ("Toolbox"), with what the scrapped ones gave. A wooden case that dropped cases counts them at the case's price; one that dropped toolboxes shows as unpriced instead of costing the case for nothing, and its tooltip tells toolboxes from a drop whose price is not read yet. A toolbox has no price history (the API answers 404), which would have stopped the history from reading at all: it is no longer asked for, and a code without history is skipped.
- **Rarity.** An image named by its item code says the rarity before the frame does. The six v0.27 frame colours are known (none was within reach of the v0.26 palette), and the palette read off the grid is kept after v0.27's Select-an-item window closes.
- **The case window** takes the case from its image before its odds; a toolbox's or a wooden case's window never gets the case panel. v0.27's window shows no image of its case, only what it drops: a wooden case's lists a case1, a toolbox and a case2, a toolbox's reel draws tools, and both print odds with Common in them as a normal case's do, so both had read as a normal case. The toolbox's line, "A random employee tool", is known too.
- **The Cases strip on an inventory** stands before the grid that holds the cases. v0.27 wraps each inventory tile two boxes deeper than v0.26, so with one kind of case in the inventory the strip went into that case's 48 px cell and stood one letter wide (seen on dev, 2026-10-18); it now takes the grid that holds the case with the other items, as two kinds of case always gave; a case alone in its grid finds v0.27's grid by its layout, and v0.26's row two boxes up as before. The game's windows are left out as well: the wooden case window shows its drops as images (read off the dev client's code, and tested on a page built to its shape), which could pull the strip off the inventory while the window was open.
- **A procedure the game removes** (404 "No procedure found") is said so and asked again in an hour, not every minute.
- **An order that trades nothing no longer fails the books.** The dev server holds buy orders at a price of 0 (bread, grain and petroleum, 99,927 to 999,999 of them), and one such order failed all 24 books the Cases strip reads ("Invalid order price or quantity"), so the strip never left "loading". An order at a price or quantity of 0 or less is now left out of its book; a price or quantity that is no number at all still fails, as a changed API.
- **`npm run build:dev`** writes a copy for testers in `dist/dev`, pointed at dev.warera.io and its API, to load unpacked beside the store build. The store build's permissions do not change.

## 3.6.0

- **Sniper crafts cost their steel.** A sniper craft (the slot picked in the game's craft window: CRAFT instead of CRAFT RANDOM) burns twice the steel of a random craft; the scraps are the same. The game's feed does not say which way a craft was made, so the Ledger charged every craft the random fee and a sniper crafter saw a profit that was not there (8 steel short on every epic craft, 16 on a legendary, 32 on a mythic). Now each press of the game's CRAFT button is noted with the window's pick, tied to the craft it made once the history reads it (the page's clock and the server's are matched by the gap most presses agree on), and written on that craft for good. Crafts the extension did not see (before it was installed, on another device) are told by a streak: six weapons or five of one armour piece in a row are too many for a random craft (a weapon comes 30% of the time, each armour slot 14%), so they are taken for sniper crafts; about half a percent of random crafts fall in such a streak by chance. Sniper crafts carry ◎ in the Ledger, its tier chips and the game's craft window, faint when a streak tells it. Settings, Advanced, How you craft: Detect (the new default), Always random, Always sniper craft. A "random" saved by an earlier version was the default every save wrote, so it becomes Detect once; a sniper craft set by hand is kept.
- **The whole history in the Ledger.** The list under the tiles holds every piece got or gone in the window, newest first, 50 a page (« ‹ Newer · 51–100 of 2,345 · Older › »), with All · Craft · Case · Buy · Loot filters and their counts. It scrolls in a box of its own that keeps its place while the numbers update. The worker sends the page one page at a time; the page still never receives the history.
- **Prices brought to the piece's stats.** A piece's value was the median of the sales alike as they came, so a top roll grouped with lower ones (similar stats: a sniper's crit ±1, a jet's attack ±19) was priced down by them and a bottom roll up. Each sale alike now counts at what it would have fetched with the piece's own stats, by what each stat is worth on that item's market, read off the item's own sales: a robust slope over pairs of sales sold within 48 hours of each other, the other stat alike, for a weapon each stat's slope read again over prices taken back by the other's; nothing while fewer than 40 pairs tell it, never a better roll priced down. On synthetic markets (`node scripts/backtest-prices.mjs`) the median error falls from 4.6% to 3.7%, for the top fifth of the roll from 5.8% to 4.7%; where stats pay nothing it moves by 0.2 points at most. The item's own median (any stats) is never adjusted and still never earns a tag. The sell price, the Ledger's held pieces and FLIP all rest on it.
- **Steadier, faster loading.** Each row's line keeps one width whatever it says, so the game's price and BUY no longer move under the cursor while the quote, the averages and the sales are read; a longer phrase ends in an ellipsis, open Details take the whole row. On the synthetic market (`node scripts/jitter.mjs`) the layout shifts while it settles fall from seven to one, the first insertion. Rows the game draws later (Load more, the offer list after its own read) get their line in the frame they first show in, so the extension adds no shift to them at all; what the game draws after the extension starts (the offer list after its own read, a dialog) is found at once, where before it waited for a scan something else set off, at worst the 30-second fallback. The first frame says READING instead of STALE and "bid depth too thin". A scan works out each row's value once instead of up to three times (scans take about 40% less time, the longest half as long). The content script starts at document_start, ahead of the game. An item's sales are brought up to date by reading only the new ones, usually one page of the API instead of ten.
- **Ready for the Chrome Web Store.** A description the store accepts (132 characters at most), `npm run package` for a reproducible store zip without the tests, a privacy policy ([docs/PRIVACY.md](docs/PRIVACY.md)) and the store listing with the privacy-practices answers ([docs/CHROME-WEB-STORE.md](docs/CHROME-WEB-STORE.md)). The release notes moved from the README to this file.

## 3.0.0

- **Your whole history, kept.** The first time a game page shows your account, the worker reads every transaction of yours back to the start of your profile (crafts, item-market buys and sales, dismantles, case openings, battle loot and resource trades, one walk of all six types) and keeps it in this browser, month by month. ## 2.1.0

- **Every piece is priced by its own stats.** A sniper with 101 attack and 16% crit is not the same goods as one with 130 and 20%, so a row's value is now the median of the last 7 days' sales of the same item whose stats sit next to the piece's: within a tenth of each stat's roll range ("same stats", three sales or more), else within a quarter ("similar stats", five or more). Each stat is measured against its own range, so one crit point weighs as much as several attack points. The span of the matched sales is printed on the row (`5 sales · atk 101–106 · crit 16% · 7 d`) and Details add their price range and the median time from listing to sale.
- **FLIP rests on those sales only.** A listing earns FLIP when pieces with these stats sold for the threshold or more above its price. The item's any-stats median and the game's average are still shown as a fallback, but neither earns a tag.
- **Every item on the page gets its sales read**, the grid's item first, 7 days back (ten pages of 100 at most), cached five minutes; nothing needs to be selected any more.
- **Weapon rows read correctly.** Live rows print the attack, the crit and the durability in that order (`101`, `16%`, `100%`), and armour its one stat and the durability (`12%`, `100%`); the reader took the first percentage as durability, which misread every weapon and called precision gloves unreadable. It now takes the last percentage before the price as durability and every number before it as a stat. Confirmed against two live rows.
- **Listed peers.** Details name how many other listings of the same stats are on the page and the cheapest of them.
